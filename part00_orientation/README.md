# Part 00 — Orientation

Three lessons that do not teach any mathematics. They teach you how to read
mathematics, and they convince you that the mathematics is worth reading.

## What this part covers

| # | Lesson | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00 | [Why Mathematics Matters in Computer Science](../part00_orientation/00_why_math_matters.md) | Eight concrete places the mathematics is already in your code: hash map lookup cost, floating point failure, neural network training, RSA, PCA, and more. No abstraction, only specifics you can check. |
| 01 | [How to Read Mathematical Notation](../part00_orientation/01_how_to_read_notation.md) | A translation table for the notation you will meet in every later part: set-builder, summation, product, quantifiers, big-O, subscripts, free versus bound variables. Each one gets a plain-English sentence and a Python equivalent. |
| 02 | [Study Plan and Prerequisites](../part00_orientation/02_study_plan.md) | Three concrete week-by-week plans (fast, thorough, interview), a 20-question self-diagnostic, and the prerequisite graph of the whole course as runnable data. |

## What this part assumes

- You can read Python. That is the only real requirement. Everything else is
  taught from zero.
- You have Python 3.11 available. Every code block in this part runs as written
  with the standard library and nothing else.
- You do **not** need calculus, linear algebra, or a proof background. Lesson 02
  tells you which of those you already have and which you should skip.

## How long it takes

| Lesson | Reading time | Doing the exercises |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00 | 15 min | 15 min |
| 01 | 25 min | 30 min |
| 02 | 15 min | 20 min |

Roughly **55 minutes** of reading, **65 minutes** of exercises, so about **two
hours** for Part 00 including thinking time. Lesson 01 is the one to slow down
on. It is the highest-value lesson in the repository: everything after it
assumes you can read a formula out loud.

## Where to go next

Finish Part 00 and start [Part 01 — Logic and Proof](../part01_logic_proof/README.md).
Do not skip Part 01. The proof technique taught there is used directly in Parts
02, 03, 05 and 06, and skipping it makes every later result something you have
to take on faith.

You can also jump straight to a specific topic using the
[lesson index](../README.md#the-11-parts). Every lesson is self-contained, so
jumping to [Lesson 40 — SVD and PCA](../part03_linear_algebra/40_svd_and_pca.md)
or [Lesson 80 — Big-O](../part06_algorithms_math/80_big_o_and_complexity.md)
works if you are comfortable with the notation from Lesson 01.

See also [SYMBOLS.md](../SYMBOLS.md) for the notation reference that Lesson 01
teaches you how to read, and [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) for how these
lessons are written.
